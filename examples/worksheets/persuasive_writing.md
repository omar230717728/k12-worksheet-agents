# English Language Arts: Writing a Persuasive Paragraph

**Grade:** 6

**Learning objective:** Write a persuasive paragraph with a clear claim, supporting reasons and evidence, and a concluding sentence.

## Explanation

Persuasive writing aims to convince a reader to agree with your viewpoint or take a specific action. A strong persuasive paragraph contains four essential building blocks: a claim, reasons, evidence with elaboration, and a concluding sentence. In addition, transition words help guide the reader smoothly from one idea to the next.

A claim is the main argument of your paragraph. Unlike a simple fact, which can be proven true immediately, or a personal preference, which is merely a matter of taste, a claim is arguable. Other people could reasonably disagree with it. For example, stating that dogs have fur is a fact, and stating that puppies are cute is a personal taste. In contrast, stating that middle school students should be permitted to adopt classroom pets is an arguable claim.

Once you state your claim, you must explain why it is valid by providing reasons. Reasons are logical statements that support your position. For every reason you give, you need concrete evidence to prove it. In student persuasive writing, evidence can include factual descriptions, real-world observations, or clearly framed realistic scenarios. Along with evidence, you must provide elaboration, which explains exactly how that piece of evidence supports your main claim.

Transition words act as bridges between your sentences, creating cohesion. Words and phrases such as 'furthermore', 'for instance', 'in addition', and 'consequently' help the reader follow your logical train of thought.

Finally, a concluding sentence wraps up the paragraph. A successful conclusion does not merely copy your opening claim word for word. Instead, it restates the main claim in a fresh, impactful way or leaves the reader with a clear call to action.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Constructing a Complete Persuasive Paragraph

Use the prompt 'Should middle schools replace printed textbooks entirely with digital tablets?' to write a unified persuasive paragraph containing a claim, a reason, evidence with elaboration, and a concluding sentence.

1. Step 1: Write an arguable claim. Avoid merely stating a preference like 'I like tablets better.' A strong claim is: 'Middle schools should replace printed textbooks with digital tablets to improve student learning and resource accessibility.'
2. Step 2: Provide a logical reason to back up the claim. A solid reason is: 'Tablets allow school materials to stay current without the massive cost of repurchasing physical books every few years.'
3. Step 3: Introduce evidence using a transition word. For example: 'For instance, digital publishers can update online science and geography texts instantly whenever new discoveries or global boundary shifts occur.'
4. Step 4: Add elaboration to connect the evidence directly back to your claim: 'This immediate access ensures that students learn the most accurate information available rather than studying from outdated pages printed a decade ago.'
5. Step 5: Write a concluding sentence that restates the main point using fresh phrasing: 'Transitioning to digital reading devices equips classrooms with dynamic, modern knowledge tools that printed volumes simply cannot match.'

**Answer:** Middle schools should replace printed textbooks with digital tablets to improve student learning and resource accessibility. Tablets allow school materials to stay current without the massive cost of repurchasing physical books every few years. For instance, digital publishers can update online science and geography texts instantly whenever new discoveries or global boundary shifts occur. This immediate access ensures that students learn the most accurate information available rather than studying from outdated pages printed a decade ago. Transitioning to digital reading devices equips classrooms with dynamic, modern knowledge tools that printed volumes simply cannot match.

## Summary

- A claim is an arguable statement asserting a stance on a specific topic, not a simple fact or personal preference.
- Reasons provide the logical 'why' behind the claim, showing the reader why your position makes sense.
- Evidence provides concrete backing for your reasons, and elaboration explains how the evidence connects back to the claim.
- Transitions such as 'furthermore', 'for example', and 'consequently' connect ideas and build paragraph cohesion.
- A concluding sentence reinforces your stance using new words or offers a call to action without merely copying the claim.

## Exercises

1. Read the three statements below and identify which one represents an arguable claim suitable for a persuasive paragraph. Explain why the other two do not qualify.
Statement A: Many middle schools require students to take physical education classes.
Statement B: Physical education is the most fun subject of the school day.
Statement C: Daily physical education classes should be mandatory for all middle school students to promote lifelong wellness habits.
2. A student is writing a paragraph arguing that schools should extend lunch periods from thirty minutes to forty-five minutes. Their stated reason is: 'A longer break allows students sufficient time to finish nutritious meals and settle down before afternoon classes.' Which piece of evidence best supports this reason?
Evidence 1: Cafeteria staff must arrive at school early in the morning to prepare food.
Evidence 2: In a typical thirty-minute period, students often spend fifteen minutes waiting in line, leaving very little time to chew their food and relax.
Evidence 3: Some schools have vending machines that sell bottled water and fruit snacks.
3. Fill in each blank with an appropriate transition word or phrase from the list to ensure the paragraph flows logically. Use each option once: (Furthermore / For instance / As a result)

Community service should be incorporated into the middle school curriculum to foster civic responsibility. [Blank 1], students who participate in neighborhood cleanups directly observe how their personal efforts improve local parks. [Blank 2], engaging in community outreach helps young people develop teamwork and communication skills. [Blank 3], introducing service projects during middle school prepares students to become proactive, thoughtful citizens.
4. Identify which essential component is missing from the following paragraph (Claim, Supporting Reason/Evidence, or Concluding Sentence), and write a sentence that could fix the problem:

Allowing students to listen to instrumental music through headphones during independent work time improves classroom focus. Many students report that quiet background melodies help block out distracting hallway chatter and desk shuffling. When background noises are minimized, students can maintain concentration on difficult reading or math tasks for longer stretches of time.
5. Write an original persuasive paragraph of 5 to 7 sentences on the following topic: 'Should middle schools assign homework over long holiday breaks?' Your paragraph must include: a clear claim, at least one reason supported by evidence and elaboration, appropriate transition words, and a concluding sentence that restates your claim with fresh wording.

## Solutions

1. Statement C is the only arguable claim because it takes a clear, debatable stance on school policy that someone could reasonably argue against. Statement A is a simple verifiable fact, not an argument. Statement B is a personal taste or opinion that cannot be debated using objective reasoning or evidence.
2. Evidence 2 is the best support. It directly illustrates the specific time crunch students face in the cafeteria, showing why thirty minutes is insufficient for eating and relaxing. Evidence 1 focuses on cafeteria staff schedules, and Evidence 3 focuses on vending machine options, neither of which supports the need for more lunch time.
3. Blank 1: For instance (introduces a specific example of neighborhood cleanups).
Blank 2: Furthermore (adds an additional reason regarding teamwork and communication).
Blank 3: As a result (shows the outcome or conclusion of introducing these projects).
4. The missing component is a Concluding Sentence. The paragraph ends abruptly on an elaboration sentence without wrapping up the argument or restating the claim in a fresh way. An appropriate concluding sentence could be: 'Permitting headphones during quiet study periods is a simple strategy that enhances student productivity and concentration.'
5. Answers will vary, but an acceptable response should follow this model: Middle schools should eliminate homework assignments over long holiday breaks so that students can truly recharge. Extended holidays provide a vital opportunity for young people to spend meaningful time with family and pursue hobbies outside the classroom. For example, students who spend breaks visiting relatives or practicing sports return to school feeling refreshed rather than burned out from late-night studying. When pupils have genuine downtime to rest their minds, they demonstrate higher enthusiasm and sharper focus once regular classes resume. Therefore, protecting holiday breaks from academic assignments is essential for maintaining student well-being and sustained academic success.
