# English Language Arts: Writing a Persuasive Paragraph

**Grade:** 6

**Learning objective:** Write a persuasive paragraph with a clear claim, supporting reasons and evidence, and a concluding sentence.

## Explanation

A persuasive paragraph tries to convince the reader to agree with your opinion. To write a strong persuasive paragraph, you need four main parts. First, you need a clear claim, which is your main argument or opinion on a topic. Second, you need supporting reasons that explain why your claim is true. Third, you need specific evidence, such as facts, examples, or statistics, to back up your reasons. Fourth, you need a concluding sentence that restates your claim in a new way and wraps up your paragraph. Transition words like 'first,' 'furthermore,' and 'in conclusion' help guide the reader smoothly from one idea to the next.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Structuring a Persuasive Paragraph

Read this paragraph and identify its key parts: Claim, Reasons, Evidence, and Conclusion. Paragraph: Middle school students should have a later start to the school day. To begin with, teenagers experience biological changes that make it difficult to fall asleep early. According to the American Academy of Pediatrics, middle and high schools should start no earlier than 8:30 a.m. to allow students to get adequate rest. Furthermore, well-rested students are more focused and perform better in their classes. Because of these health and academic benefits, school boards should adjust morning schedules for teens.

1. Find the main opinion or argument of the paragraph, which is the claim.
2. Locate the statements that explain why the author holds this opinion, which are the reasons.
3. Look for facts, data, or expert statements that support the reasons, which is the evidence.
4. Identify the final sentence that summarizes the main point and provides closure, which is the conclusion.

**Answer:** Claim: Middle school students should have a later start to the school day. Reasons: Teenagers experience biological changes affecting sleep, and well-rested students perform better. Evidence: The American Academy of Pediatrics states schools should start no earlier than 8:30 a.m. Conclusion: Because of these health and academic benefits, school boards should adjust morning schedules for teens.

## Summary

- A persuasive paragraph starts with a clear claim that states your opinion.
- Reasons explain why your claim is correct, while evidence provides facts and examples to back up those reasons.
- A strong concluding sentence restates the claim and wraps up the paragraph.
- Transition words help connect your ideas smoothly and logically.

## Exercises

1. Read the following sentence: 'Schools should ban the sale of soda in vending machines.' Is this sentence a claim, a reason, or evidence?
2. Match the following statement to its category: 'A study by the Health Department found that students who drink soda daily are twice as forthright to experience tooth decay.' Is this a reason or evidence?
3. Read this incomplete paragraph: 'Every classroom should have a class pet. First, pets teach students responsibility because children must feed them and clean their cages. In addition, caring for an animal can lower stress levels during difficult school days.' Write a strong concluding sentence for this paragraph.
4. Draft a complete persuasive paragraph on the topic of whether physical education should be required every day. Make sure to include a clear claim, at least one reason with evidence, and a concluding sentence.

## Solutions

1. Claim
2. Evidence
3. In conclusion, adding a classroom pet is a wonderful way to foster student responsibility and create a calming learning environment.
4. Physical education should be required every day for all students. To begin with, daily physical activity promotes lifelong health habits and combats childhood obesity. According to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, children need at least 60 minutes of physical activity daily to develop properly. Furthermore, active students tend to have better concentration and higher test scores in their academic subjects. Therefore, daily gym classes are essential for keeping students both physically healthy and mentally sharp.
