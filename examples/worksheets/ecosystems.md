# Science: Food Chains and Ecosystems

**Grade:** 5

**Learning objective:** Identify producers, consumers, and decomposers; explain how energy moves through a simple food chain; and predict one effect when an organism is removed.

## Explanation

An ecosystem is a community of living things interacting with their nonliving environment. Every ecosystem needs an energy source, which starts with the Sun. Producers, such as plants and algae, use sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide to make their own food through photosynthesis. Because they produce their own food, they form the base of every food chain. Consumers are living things that cannot make their own food and must eat other organisms for energy. Consumers are grouped by what they eat: herbivores eat only plants, carnivores eat only other animals, and omnivores eat both plants and animals. Decomposers, like fungi and bacteria, break down dead plants and animals, returning important nutrients back to the soil for producers to use again. A food chain shows how energy moves in a straight line from the Sun to producers, and then to various consumers, with arrows pointing in the direction that the energy flows. Because all organisms in an ecosystem are connected, removing even one organism can disrupt the entire chain.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Analyzing a Forest Food Chain

Look at this forest food chain: Grass -> Rabbit -> Fox. Identify the producer and consumers, explain the direction of the energy arrows, and predict what happens to the fox if a disease removes all the rabbits.

1. Identify the producer at the start of the food chain, which is the grass because it makes its own food using sunlight.
2. Identify the consumers. The rabbit is a primary consumer and herbivore that eats the grass. The fox is a secondary consumer and carnivore that eats the rabbit.
3. Understand the arrows. The arrows show that energy moves from the grass to the rabbit, and then from the rabbit to the fox.
4. Predict the effect of removing the rabbit. Since foxes rely on rabbits for food, the fox population would decrease due to a lack of food unless they could find another food source.

**Answer:** Grass is the producer, the rabbit is a herbivore consumer, and the fox is a carnivore consumer. The arrows show energy moving from grass to rabbit to fox. If rabbits are removed, the fox population will decrease because they lose their main source of energy.

## Summary

- Producers make their own food using sunlight through photosynthesis.
- Consumers get energy by eating other living things and are classified as herbivores, carnivores, or omnivores.
- Decomposers break down dead material and recycle nutrients into the soil.
- Food chains show the linear flow of energy, with arrows pointing in the direction the energy travels.
- Removing a single organism from a food chain causes cascading effects that impact all other connected organisms.

## Exercises

1. Classify each of the following organisms as a producer, a consumer, or a decomposer: oak tree, mushroom, and hawk.
2. In a pond ecosystem, small fish eat algae, and herons eat small fish. Write this out as a food chain using correct text arrows to show the flow of energy.
3. Explain whether a rabbit is a herbivore, carnivore, or omnivore, and describe its role in a food chain.
4. Consider the food chain: Berry bush -> Mouse -> Snake -> Owl. What would likely happen to the population of snakes if a drought kills off most of the berry bushes?
5. What is the primary role of decomposers like bacteria and fungi in an ecosystem?

## Solutions

1. Oak tree is a producer; mushroom is a decomposer; hawk is a consumer.
2. Algae -> Small fish -> Heron
3. A rabbit is a herbivore because it eats only plants. In a food chain, it acts as a consumer that gets its energy directly from producers.
4. If berry bushes die, the mouse population will decrease due to lack of food. With fewer mice to eat, the snake population will also decrease because their energy source is disappearing.
5. Decomposers break down dead plants and animals, returning essential nutrients to the soil so producers can use them.
